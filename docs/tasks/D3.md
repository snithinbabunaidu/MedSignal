## Goal

A written script for a 20-minute demo, rehearsed once from a clean checkout. The first half shows the app as the pharmacy safety officer uses it; the second half shows what is under it. Either engineer can present any part, because each reviewed the other's work.

## Context

- Read `README.md` section "What it does" (her three questions), `docs/tasks/A3.md` (the five pages), `docs/architecture-note.md`, `docs/acceptance-report.md`, `docs/optimization-log.md`, `docs/runbook.md` if D4 has merged, and `docs/tasks/A6.md` (Docker Compose is the fallback).
- Her three questions, in her words: which drugs have adverse events being reported that the label does not mention; which have an active recall; what has changed since yesterday.
- Show real behaviour only. Nothing is mocked, and every number said aloud is in the acceptance report.
- FAERS changes quarterly, so "since yesterday" is mostly recalls and label updates. Say so; the app says so too.
- Presenters follow the ownership in `docs/project-plan.md`: Nithin for FAERS, identity, signals, runs, checks, optimization and backtest; Zenish for labels, extraction, recalls, change feed and the app.

## Inputs

- A machine with the full data loaded and published, and the Docker Compose sample as fallback.
- One prepared new label version or recall to load live (the replay data of C4 or C3).
- One prepared input that makes a quality check fail (see C5's tests for a way to cause one).

## Outputs

`docs/demo-script.md` with these sections: Setup before the demo; Outline; Script (for each block: what to click or run, what to say in two or three sentences, what the audience should see); Fallbacks; Questions to prepare.

## Files to create

- `docs/demo-script.md`.
- `docs/results/D3.md`: rehearsal date, commit, measured time per block, what went wrong, what was changed.

## Implementation steps

1. Pick the example drug and reaction once and use them throughout (an SGLT2 inhibitor and ketoacidosis if the data shows it). Write the exact `rxcui`, term and URLs into the script.
2. Write each block with the exact command or click path. Commands are copied from `docs/runbook.md`, not retyped.
3. Setup section: clean checkout, build, data state, app started, browser tabs opened in order, terminal font size, the two prepared inputs in place, and how to reset them after a rehearsal.
4. Fallbacks: if the live daily run fails, show a saved terminal log and move on; if the machine fails, run `docker compose up` on the other laptop; screenshots of every page are stored outside the repository.
5. Questions to prepare, each with an answer of at most three sentences and the name of who answers. At least these:
   - Why not Foundry? Why no LLM for reading labels, and what does the rule-based reader miss?
   - Is a signal proof that the drug causes the reaction?
   - Why PRR with chi-square, and why rank by the lower bound of the odds ratio?
   - How do you know the incremental result equals a full rebuild?
   - What share of drug names did not resolve, and what happens to them?
   - How is a case counted once when FAERS sends follow-ups?
   - What is the extraction precision and recall, and on how many labels?
   - How long does a full rebuild take, and what was the biggest speed-up?
   - Why three modules and two Scala versions? What did TableParser and XmlDoc each do?
   - Which criteria were not met, and why?
   - What would you do with another month?
6. Rehearse end to end from a fresh `git clone` on the demo machine, with a timer and the other engineer as audience. Record the time of each block. If the total passes 20 minutes, cut content, not speaking pace. Then swap roles for the question list.
